24.
The Field Crew Was Working Even When the Commander Wasn't Watching.

When I was building my second website, I mainly checked everything on my computer.

I checked how the screen looked,

changed button positions,

and checked the images.

Once the site was mostly built, I opened it on my phone.

The site appeared.

Huh?

It looked pretty good.

Everything was arranged properly for the screen size.

The buttons fit.

The images looked pretty good, too.

I thought I'd been working mainly on the PC version,

but the mobile screen had been taken into account during the process as well.

Of course, it wasn't perfect.

Later, I found a few problems that only appeared on phones.

I had been acting like a commander, giving instructions based only on what I could see on my computer,

while out in the field, work was getting done in places I wasn't even watching.

Come to think of it,

I vaguely remembered seeing ChatGPT include various instructions about how things should look on mobile when it prepared modification instructions.

I didn't need to know everything ChatGPT and Codex were doing to build it.

I just had to look at the result.
If there was a problem, I could tell them to fix it.

Does a commander need to know what's happening at every single place in the field?

Isn't it enough to set the overall direction

and check the results?

I was gradually getting used to work being done even when I didn't know exactly what was happening.
